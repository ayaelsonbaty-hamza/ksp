What is happening?

Ions break off the surface of the solid and spread into the water. Some ions bump back into the solid and re-join it. At first dissolving is faster. As more ions build up in the water, re-joining speeds up until the two rates are equal. The solution is now saturated: this is a dynamic equilibrium, and the ionic product equals Ksp.

Press Add …⁻ ions to add more of one ion (a common ion). Q goes above Ksp, so extra solid forms until Q falls back to Ksp.

What does a high or low Ksp look like?

Three XY salts start with the same amount of solid. The bigger the Ksp, the more ions can stay dissolved before the solution is saturated.

Sodium chloride, NaCl

Ksp ≈ 37Ions dissolved: 0

Very high Ksp. All the solid dissolves: very soluble.

Calcium sulfate, CaSO₄

Ksp = 4.9 × 10⁻⁵Ions dissolved: 0

Medium Ksp. Some dissolves, then it saturates: slightly soluble.

Silver chloride, AgCl

Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰Ions dissolved: 0

Very low Ksp. Almost nothing dissolves: insoluble.

Careful when the formula type is different

Ag₂CrO₄ has a smaller Ksp (1.1 × 10⁻¹²) than AgCl (1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰), but it is actually more soluble. Its Ksp has units of mol³ dm⁻⁹, not mol² dm⁻⁶, because three ions are multiplied.

Rule: only compare Ksp values directly when the compounds have the same formula type (XY with XY).
